Step 01 / Problem & Context

From bond listings to a trading desk

The Problem: Harmoney launched as a bond listing website — useful for discovery, but traders still ran their actual workflow across four to six separate screens (market watch, order book, execution, settlement).

The Bet: Consolidate the highest-frequency trading actions into one platform so institutional traders, wealth firms, and brokers could execute without context-switching — and Harmoney could earn a permanent place in their daily stack.

Step 02 / What I Did

Scope covered end-to-end UX from discovery through launch: offline trading workflow mapping, product structure, key screens for web and mobile, and translation of complex trading logic into clear interactions.

The design foundation was built from scratch — tokens, reusable components, and handoff specs — with ongoing alignment alongside the CEO, CTO, and engineers on scope decisions, usability feedback, and release QA.

Understanding the Product

At launch, Harmoney functioned as a bond-search site while trading still ran offline through calls and manual follow-ups. The target state was one connected workflow: discover bonds, compare options, place orders, and track execution without leaving the platform.

The Problem

Bond Trading Was Broken

Bond trades were still closed over phone calls — brokers negotiated verbally, hung up, and recorded the deal manually in a spreadsheet. If a price moved while a broker was mid-call with someone else, there was no way to know. Wealth managers had no visibility into what bonds were even available.

The CEO, who came from the bond trading industry, knew this could be digitized. The design challenge was different from most consumer products: institutional traders optimize for speed and data density, not visual polish.

Reflections

Key takeaways from the project

As the sole designer at an early-stage startup, there was no design manager or design ops function to lean on — every decision had to hold up on its own.

01 · Learn the domain first

Design decisions stayed guesses until bond trading mechanics were understood — not a surface read, but the actual workflows the CEO was building around. Domain fluency, not visual polish, made the interaction design defensible.

02 · Systems outlast screens

No single designer can specify every state of every component by hand. Building a reusable system — tokens, components, patterns — let engineering extrapolate correctly to states not personally drawn.

03 · Ship, then correct with real usage

Harmoney launched with a deliberately scoped first version, then improved based on how real traders actually used it. That feedback loop produced a better product faster than holding the release for a more complete design would have.
