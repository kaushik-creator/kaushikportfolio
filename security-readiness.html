Case Study · Druva

Six settings scattered.
One readiness score.

Druva's Cloud Administrators were forced to jump across six separate platform settings pages just to verify basic security hygiene like MFA and Geofencing. I designed the Cyber Resiliency Readiness Score—a consolidated, high-signal dashboard widget that surfaces missing security controls at a glance and allows admins to fix gaps in-place without losing context.

Target Persona

Meet Jordan — Cloud Administrator

Jordan manages the organization's Druva tenant as part of a broader IT operations role. Jordan isn't a dedicated security analyst, but is periodically on the hook with internal InfoSec teams to prove baseline controls are actually configured. Jordan doesn't have time to audit settings pages—they need an instant answer to what is misconfigured and a fast path to remediate it.

Every time Jordan opens the Security Center, they are asking three things:

  1. Is my environment actually protected right now?
  2. Did anything suspicious happen since I last checked?
  3. What's the fastest thing I can fix to reduce risk today?

Chapter 1

Chapter 1: The six-page compliance hunt

Configuring baseline security—Multi-Factor Authentication, Single Sign-On, Geofencing, and Alert Subscriptions—required navigating to six completely separate platform pages. Because settings were scattered, administrators frequently missed key configurations, leaving tenants exposed during compliance audits.

Our goal was simple: consolidate these disparate configurations into a single, high-visibility anchor widget that highlights misconfigurations and provides a one-click path to fix them.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2: Anchoring the Security Center

The existing Security Center was reactive—answering "did a threat occur?" or "is a backup job failing?". It lacked a proactive view answering: "How secure is my environment right now, and what should I fix today?"

We anchored the new Readiness Score in the top-left position of the dashboard. The primary design challenge was ensuring audit-blocking mandatory controls (like MFA) were visually distinguished from recommended risk-reducing controls (like Geofencing) without overwhelming the rest of the page layout.

The missing piece

A top-left anchor widget answering: "What is the fastest thing I can fix to reduce risk today?"

The challenge

Visually differentiating Mandatory (audit-blocking) from Recommended (risk-reducing) controls without fighting the rest of the dashboard for dominance.

Chapter 3

Chapter 3: Distinguishing Mandatory vs. Recommended controls

We iterated through three visual models to solve one core tension: mandatory controls and recommended controls cannot share equal visual weight.

Iteration 1 — Side-by-side split donuts

Used two separate donut charts side by side — one for Required (Mandatory) and one for Optional (Recommended).

Why it failed: Placing both charts at equal scale implied recommended controls held equal operational urgency to mandatory audit blockers.
Equal weight implied false urgency balance

Iteration 2 — Single arc gauge with list

Used a unified semi-circular gauge with equal arc segments for all 7 items, plus a static breakdown list.

Why it failed: It obscured the mandatory distinction, relying solely on small asterisks in an adjacent text list.
Equal arc segments hid the mandatory distinction

Iteration 3 — Concentric nested arcs

Introduced an inner arc for Mandatory progress and an outer arc for Recommended progress.

Why it failed as a standalone card: Displaying the full checklist alongside the double-ring graphic crammed too much visual noise into a single dashboard widget.
Double ring + full checklist overcrowded the card

Chapter 4

Chapter 4: The Shipped Experience — Gauge, List, and In-Context Edit

The shipped widget resolves the core tension: the inner arc tracks Mandatory progress, while the outer arc tracks Recommended progress. A Gauge/List toggle lets Jordan switch between a macro compliance view and a granular action list.

Gauge view

Nested concentric arcs — inner ring tracks Mandatory (audit-blocking) controls; outer ring tracks Recommended (risk-reducing) controls. Hover tooltip surfaces a specific gap; the pencil affordance opens in-context edit without leaving the page.

List view

Full control checklist with pass/fail status, Mandatory asterisks, subscription minimums, and a pencil edit icon on each row so Jordan can remediate without hopping to six settings pages.

Control breakdown

Mandatory *

Audit-blocking controls

  • Multi-Factor Authentication
  • Cyber Resiliency Reports (minimum 3 subscriptions)
  • Cyber Resiliency Alerts (minimum 3 subscriptions)

Recommended

Risk-reducing controls

  • Single Sign-On
  • Geofencing
  • Restore Scan

Key design decisions

01

Gauge / List toggle

Supports two distinct mental modes—scanning the high-level score at a glance (gauge view) or reviewing specific failed controls (list view).

02

In-context configuration modals

Clicking the edit icon opens configuration flows directly in a local modal. Admins fix gaps on the spot without leaving the Security Center or losing page state.

03

Macro to micro hierarchy

Arranged the dashboard top-to-bottom from macro readiness (top) down to active risk triage and threat hunting (bottom).

Outcomes

Key Outcomes & Lessons

01

Zero-navigation remediation

Admins no longer hop across six settings pages. In-context modals reduced friction, leading to higher baseline security adoption across tenant accounts.

02

Clear audit signals

Separating mandatory controls into their own nested arc gave admins an immediate answer when preparing for internal compliance reviews.

03

Toggle flexibility

Providing both a visual gauge and a granular list satisfied both executive-level scanning and admin-level execution on a single card.
