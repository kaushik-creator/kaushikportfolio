Druva — provisioning & access for service partners

Druva, its partners (MSPs), and end customers share one system for turning products on and controlling who can see them. I designed the provisioning and access model that let partners offer more products — without breaking licensing rules or exposing a customer's data to their partner.

Role
UX Designer
Domain
Backup & Cybersecurity
Timeline
Feb – Aug 2025
Type
Enterprise · B2B
PM Support UX Designer FE / BE Dev
Context

Druva — cloud-native backup and data security for endpoints, SaaS, data centers, and cloud workloads.

MSP — an IT partner managing backup and data protection for many clients from one dashboard.

The question

Research question — where do the partner, the customer, and Druva's own needs conflict, and can one access model serve all three?

Method

I started from where the three parties collided: MSP onboarding and provisioning calls, Druva's licensing docs and Support Slack threads, and sessions with PMs and engineering. The pattern: the same action — provisioning a product — followed different rules depending on who started it and who could see the result.

Partner

Wants more access, to sell and manage more.

Customer

Wants less exposure; won't show its reports to the partner.

Druva

Wants less manual provisioning work.

Synthesis

Before it was a clean model it was a mess — three actor types, overlapping workflows, and edge cases where provisioning, access and privacy rules contradicted each other.

Scoped out: automated bulk migration of legacy customers — manual path shipped first because licensing validation required per-customer review.

Decisions

Decision 1 — Legibility

Research showed partners couldn't read state at a glance → I designed four states as badges on the customer list; invalid actions disabled with the reason, not hidden.

Decision 2 — Irreversibility

Support tickets flagged accidental migrations → marking a tenant irreversible uses typed confirmation; invalid conversions blocked before the click, not errored after.

Decision 3 — The privacy standoff

Restricted customers give no standing access → partner requests a 24/48/72h window, customer admin holds the veto, access auto-revokes; limit enforced at the API layer, not just the button.

Scoped out: full Limited-Admin role views were specced but not designed this round — four-state model and temporary access took priority.

Outcome

Before, a partner hit a wall the moment a customer needed a product the console didn't support — they lost the deal or waited on a manual workaround. After, partners expand a customer's catalogue themselves and manage every access state from one place.

  • Self-serve expansion — partners provision advanced workloads directly, no support ticket per addition.
  • Easier management — four access states read at a glance; irreversible/invalid conversions handled deliberately.
  • Business result — by removing the provisioning ceiling, the design helped MSC bring on more partner customers and drove higher product adoption.

Validated qualitatively with Support and PMs post-launch; growth and adoption are directional outcomes the design enabled, not metrics I measured in isolation.
